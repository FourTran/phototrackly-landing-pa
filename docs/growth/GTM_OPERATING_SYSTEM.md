# PhotoTrackly GTM Operating System

**Updated:** 2026-10-07  
**Project:** PhotoTrackly  
**Current public domain:** https://phototrackly.com  
**Target markets:** United States and Australia  
**Product state:** pre-launch / in development

## Purpose

Turn PhotoTrackly growth work into one evidence loop:

```
market evidence
  -> qualified prospects
  -> outreach / distribution
  -> replies and conversations
  -> interviews and observed pain
  -> product + messaging decisions
  -> website/content changes
  -> measured acquisition results
```

SEO is one acquisition channel inside this loop, not the whole go-to-market strategy.

## Data separation

Do not put prospect email addresses, phone numbers, private conversation notes, suppression decisions, interview recordings, or individual outreach histories into this public repository. Keep those in the private CRM or another approved private source.

Public-repository documents may contain public market/company research when useful, but external-facing claims must still be reverified.

## Evidence states

Use these labels when turning research into decisions:

- **VERIFIED CURRENT** — checked against a live source in the current work cycle.
- **FIRST-PARTY** — stated directly by a prospect/customer/operator.
- **HISTORICAL RESEARCH** — previously researched, requires freshness check.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — plausible but not validated by first-party evidence.
- **UNKNOWN** — do not convert into a public claim.

## Execution rules

1. Reverify a prospect before sending.
2. Personalize from evidence, not guessed private facts.
3. First outreach should seek workflow learning, not pretend PhotoTrackly is finished.
4. Record actual sends/replies/conversations in the CRM; prepared copy is not activity.
5. Record customer language and counterexamples, not only positive signals.
6. Do not turn community participation into disguised product promotion.
7. Do not publish old `qrtrackly.com` PhotoTrackly URLs.
8. Use current GSC evidence when deciding whether SEO work needs a new page, an existing-page improvement, distribution, or simply more crawl time.
9. Do not infer product-market fit from traffic, an isolated positive reply, or one interview.
10. Keep product promises aligned with what is actually planned or approved.
